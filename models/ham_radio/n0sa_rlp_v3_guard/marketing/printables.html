A small magnetic cover for the N0SA RLP V3 Morse-code paddle. It slips over the finger pieces to keep them covered in a field bag, then pulls off when you are ready to operate. The guard prints in one piece and attaches to the key’s existing base magnets.

About the paddle

The RLP V3 is Larry Naumann’s (N0SA) lightweight iambic paddle for portable amateur radio. Used with an electronic keyer, one lever requests dots and the other requests dashes. It is a compact key for Parks on the Air, Summits on the Air and other outings where pack space matters.

N0SA’s original QRZ listing gives the key’s size as 72 × 35 × 25 mm and weight as 48 g. It has a Richlite body, adjustable magnetic return, four precision ball bearings and a 3.5 mm stereo jack. The four magnets in its base are for mounting; this guard lines up with the front two. All of the key’s magnets stay in place.

What you need

  • One printed guard: rlp_v3_guard.stl.
  • Two neodymium disc magnets: 6 mm diameter, approximately 3 mm thick, magnetized through the thickness.
  • A tiny amount of super glue suitable for the printed plastic and magnet coating.

The pockets are 6.20 mm wide and 2.95 mm deep. They are sized around discs measuring 2.96 mm thick, so measure your magnets before installing them. A true 3.00 mm thick disc would stand 0.05 mm proud; adjust pocket depth if needed. Use metric 6 mm magnets: a true ¼-inch disc is 6.35 mm and will not fit.

Fit and glue the magnets

Prototype: dry-fit the complete guard before gluing. The roof should clear the button head screws as the guard slides on, and both levers should move freely when it is seated.

  1. Slide the empty guard onto the key, checking clearance over the top screw heads, until both small stops meet the stationary body.
  2. Clear any loose printing strands or debris from the magnet pockets.
  3. Before inserting either magnet, check that it attracts its matching magnet on the key. Mark the face that will point toward the key; the opposite face goes into the pocket.
  4. Put a tiny amount of glue in each center recess, then press the magnet onto the surrounding flat seat. Wipe away squeeze-out and let it cure with the guard off the key.
  5. Check that the finished guard attaches and comes off smoothly, then check retention with gentle handling before packing it in a bag.

The shallow center recess gives glue a little space while the flat seating ring sets the magnet depth. Check polarity before pressing the magnets in: the pockets have closed bottoms, and a seated magnet can be difficult to remove even without glue.

The thicker bottom makes room for the magnets, while the thinner top and sides keep the cover compact. The 1.20 mm backing is a conservative design choice, not a tested minimum.

The inside floor stays flat. The roof slopes gently over the finger pieces, with 0.50 mm of nominal headroom at the nose centerline and about 0.59 mm at the paddle-tip reference.

Source and compatibility

Designed for the N0SA RLP V3. Check the dimensions before trying another paddle. The source lets you adjust the opening, magnet pockets, backing and nose clearance; change individual dimensions rather than scaling the whole cover.
