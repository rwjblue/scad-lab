A small magnetic cover for the N0SA RLP V3 Morse-code paddle. It slips over the finger pieces to keep them covered in a field bag, then pulls off when you are ready to operate. The guard prints in one piece and attaches to the key’s existing base magnets.

About the paddle

The RLP V3 is Larry Naumann’s (N0SA) lightweight iambic paddle for portable amateur radio. Used with an electronic keyer, one lever requests dots and the other requests dashes. It is a compact key for Parks on the Air, Summits on the Air and other outings where pack space matters.

N0SA’s original QRZ listing gives the key’s size as 72 × 35 × 25 mm and weight as 48 g. It has a Richlite body, adjustable magnetic return, four precision ball bearings and a 3.5 mm stereo jack. The four magnets in its base are for mounting; this guard lines up with the front two. All of the key’s magnets stay in place.

What you need

  • One printed guard: rlp_v3_guard.stl.
  • Two neodymium disc magnets: 6 mm diameter, approximately 3 mm thick, magnetized through the thickness.
  • A tiny amount of super glue suitable for the printed plastic and magnet coating.

The pockets are 6.20 mm wide and 2.95 mm deep. They are sized around discs measuring 2.96 mm thick, so check your magnets before gluing. A true 3.00 mm thick disc would stand 0.05 mm proud; adjust pocket depth if needed. Use metric 6 mm magnets: a true ¼-inch disc is 6.35 mm and will not fit. The optional gauge lets you try the seats and push the magnets back out afterward.

Printing

Start with PETG, a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.20 mm layers and three walls. Print at 100% scale with supports off. The full guard is already nose-down: put the closed end on the build plate and leave the open end facing up. This keeps the roof from becoming a wide bridge. Check the small stops and sideways magnet pockets in the sliced preview.

The guard occupies about 41.67 × 32.57 mm on the plate and stands 41.47 mm tall. For the optional depth strip, use 0.10 mm layers and keep its edge-down orientation; pocket depth runs horizontally so the small differences remain distinct.

Fit and glue the magnets

Prototype: dry-fit the complete guard before gluing. Both levers should move freely, with clearance around the tips, roof and sides.

  1. Slide the empty guard onto the key until both small stops meet the stationary body.
  2. Try both magnets without glue. Clear out loose printing strands so each magnet can sit fully in its pocket.
  3. Check attraction to each matching key magnet separately, and mark the face that goes into the pocket.
  4. Put a tiny amount of glue in each center recess, then press the magnet onto the surrounding flat seat. Wipe away squeeze-out and let it cure with the guard off the key.
  5. Check that the finished guard attaches and comes off smoothly, then check retention with gentle handling before packing it in a bag.

The shallow center recess gives glue a little space while the flat seating ring sets the magnet depth. The full guard has closed pocket bottoms. The fit tools have 2 mm push-out holes, so you can recover test magnets with a pin.

The thicker bottom makes room for the magnets, while the thinner top and sides keep the cover compact. The 1.20 mm backing is a conservative design choice, not a tested minimum. The depth strip compares 2.50, 2.65, 2.80, 2.95 and 3.00 mm seats.

Source and compatibility

Designed for the N0SA RLP V3. Check the dimensions before trying another paddle. The source lets you adjust the opening, magnet pockets, backing and nose clearance; change individual dimensions rather than scaling the whole cover.
